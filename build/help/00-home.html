Clock E-boxes in brain genes

In this project you will look at whether common human genetic variants disrupt the DNA sequences the body clock uses to time brain genes, and whether those sequences appear to be protected by evolution. Everything runs in this web page: a terminal, Python and R notebooks, gene databases and a genome browser. You don't need to install anything.

The question in brief

The clock proteins CLOCK and BMAL1 work as a pair, switching genes on by binding a short DNA sequence called an E-box (CACGTG). In the promoters of genes that cause epilepsy and brain “channelopathies”, how often do human variants destroy or create an E-box? Are those variants rare or common? And do E-boxes sit at positions that have changed unusually little during evolution?

Work through the parts in order. Each page explains what to do, and most steps come with a box of code and a button that runs it in the terminal or adds it to your notebook. You don't have to type everything yourself, though you will learn more if you do. Read the code before you run it, and look carefully at what comes back.
