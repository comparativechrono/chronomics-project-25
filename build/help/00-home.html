Clock E-boxes in brain genes

A guided research project. You will ask whether common human genetic variants disturb the switches the body clock uses to time brain genes — and whether those switches look protected by evolution. Everything runs in this page: a terminal, notebooks in Python and R, gene databases and a genome browser. Nothing to install.

The question, in one sentence

The clock protein pair CLOCK:BMAL1 turns genes on by binding a short piece of DNA called an E-box (CACGTG). In the promoters of genes that cause epilepsy and brain “channelopathies”, how often do human variants break or make an E-box, are those variants rare or common, and do E-boxes sit at positions that evolution has kept unusually still?

Work through the parts in order. Each page tells you what to do and has ▶ buttons that type a command into the terminal or drop code into a notebook for you — read it, run it, and look at what comes back. There is no need to type everything from scratch, but you will understand more if you try.

If you are new to all of this

That is expected. Start at The research question for the biology, then Your workbench to learn the tools on a couple of small examples. After that, Part 1 begins the real work. You can always search this guide (top left) for a word like promoter, bcftools or phyloP.
