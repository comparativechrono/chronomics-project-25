The gene lists

Before you can look for E-boxes you need to know which genes to look at, and exactly where each one starts. That is what Part 1 is for.

Where the lists come from

Deciding which genes cause a disease is a job for experts. Genomics England's PanelApp collects their judgements as gene panels, in which each gene is rated by the strength of the evidence and green means well established (). We took the green genes from two panels and saved them for you:

  • /data/examples/genes/brain_channelopathy.tsv: the 21 channelopathy genes you will look at most closely.
  • /data/examples/genes/epilepsy_all.tsv: all 641 genes, which you will use for the statistics. This is the 632 green genes of the epilepsy panel plus the 21 channelopathy genes, with each gene listed once (some are on both panels) and one mitochondrial gene left out because it has no nuclear promoter. The panel column says channelopathy for the 21 and epilepsy for the other 620.

Read the gene lists

In a Python notebook, load both lists:

import pandas as pd
chan = pd.read_csv("/data/examples/genes/brain_channelopathy.tsv", sep="\t")
epi  = pd.read_csv("/data/examples/genes/epilepsy_all.tsv", sep="\t")
print("channelopathy genes:", len(chan))
print("epilepsy genes:", len(epi))
chan

Each row has the gene symbol, its Ensembl ID, the chromosome and strand, the TSS (transcription start site) and the promoter window we will use. The strand matters. A gene on the - strand is read from right to left along the chromosome, so its promoter lies on the higher-coordinate side of the TSS. The file already takes this into account.

Where are the genes?

A quick plot of which chromosomes the genes are on:

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
counts = epi["chrom"].value_counts()
counts.plot(kind="bar", figsize=(9,3))
plt.ylabel("genes"); plt.title("Epilepsy-panel genes by chromosome"); plt.tight_layout()

Check one gene against the live database

It is good practice to check a file against its original source. Use the Databases tab to look up SCN1A in Ensembl, and check that the chromosome and strand match the table.

Think about

The channelopathy set has 21 genes and the full list has 641. Why keep both? When you later ask whether E-boxes are more common than chance, which set will give you a more reliable answer, and why? (Hint: how many E-boxes would you expect to see at all in 21 short promoters?)
