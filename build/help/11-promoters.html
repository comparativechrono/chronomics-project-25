Promoters and coordinates

An E-box is only a circadian switch if it sits in the right place — the promoter. Here you learn how genomic positions are written down, and how to pull a promoter's DNA out of the reference genome.

The promoter window

For every gene we defined the promoter as the window from 1000 letters before the TSS to 500 after — 1501 letters in all (the 1000 before, the TSS itself, and the 500 after), measured along the direction the gene is read. Those windows are saved as a BED file, the standard way to write "a stretch of a chromosome":

head /data/examples/genes/promoters.bed
Reading a BED line

chr2 166127519 166129020 SCN1A 0 - is the line for gene SCN1A, on chromosome 2 and the - strand. BED counts from 0 and its end is "one past the last base", so this window runs from base 166,127,520 to base 166,129,020 in the usual 1-based counting, and its length is simply end − start = 1501 bases. Many bioinformatics mistakes are off-by-one errors from mixing the two ways of counting — watch for it. (On the - strand the gene is read right to left, so the 1000 bases "before" its TSS, 166,128,020, are the ones to its right.)

Pull out a promoter's sequence

We also saved the DNA of every promoter as a FASTA file, promoters.fa, one record per gene. samtools faidx fetches one by name:

samtools faidx /data/examples/reference/promoters.fa SCN1A

samtools faidx labels what it prints with the name only (>SCN1A). The header line in the file itself says more — where the sequence came from: the chromosome, the genomic start and end (1-based), the strand and the TSS. Look at it with grep:

grep "^>SCN1A " /data/examples/reference/promoters.fa

You will need that genomic start later to turn a position within the promoter into a position on the chromosome. (The sequence is always the forward strand of the chromosome, left to right, whichever strand the gene is on.)

How GC-rich are these promoters?

Promoters near a TSS are often rich in C and G. That matters for E-boxes, because CACGTG is itself C/G-heavy. Measure it with seqtk:

seqtk comp /data/examples/reference/promoters.fa | awk '{gc+=$4+$5; tot+=$2} END{printf "overall GC = %.1f%%\n", 100*gc/tot}'

Keep that number in mind: when you ask whether E-boxes are "more common than chance", chance has to mean chance given this GC content, not a flat 25% per letter.

Checkpoint

There are 641 promoters (the 21 channelopathy and 632 epilepsy genes, de-duplicated, with one mitochondrial gene dropped), each 1501 bases long, and the overall GC content is about 59%. If your numbers match, your reference and your coordinates line up and you are ready to hunt for E-boxes.
