Human variation in the promoters

The 1000 Genomes Project sequenced the genomes of thousands of people from around the world. You have every variant it found in your promoters, so you can ask how common each one is, and in which populations.

The data

We extracted every single-base variant (SNV) in the 641 promoters from the 1000 Genomes 30× data, which covers 3,202 genomes from 26 populations (), and saved them in one small file:

zcat /data/examples/variants/promoter_variants.vcf.gz | grep -v "^##" | head -5

This is a VCF file, the standard format for variants. Each line is one variant, with its chromosome and position, the reference base (REF), the alternative base (ALT) and an INFO field. We have put the useful numbers in INFO: GENE, the overall allele frequency AF, and the frequency in each of the five super-populations (AF_AFR, AF_AMR, AF_EAS, AF_EUR and AF_SAS).

Allele frequency

The allele frequency is the fraction of chromosomes in a group that carry the alternative base. AF=0.5 means half of them; AF=0.0003 means it was seen on only a couple of chromosomes. A variant can be common in one population and absent from another, so a single global figure can be misleading. That is why the file keeps the super-populations separate.

Count and summarise with bcftools

bcftools is the standard tool for working with VCF files. How many variants are there, and how many are rare?

bcftools view -H /data/examples/variants/promoter_variants.vcf.gz | wc -l
# how many are rare (overall AF < 0.5%)?
bcftools query -f '%INFO/AF\n' /data/examples/variants/promoter_variants.vcf.gz \
  | awk '$1<0.005{r++} END{print r" rare of "NR}'
Checkpoint

There are 22,836 SNVs across the 641 promoters, and most of them are rare. Having many more rare variants than common ones is normal for functional regions, and it will matter again in Part 4, when you test for selection.

Load the variants into a notebook

To join the variants to your E-boxes in the next step, load them into Python as a table. A notebook line that starts with ! runs a terminal command, so here bcftools query writes the columns you need to a plain table in your home folder, and pandas reads it in:

!bcftools query -f '%CHROM\t%POS\t%ID\t%REF\t%ALT\t%INFO/GENE\t%INFO/AF\t%INFO/AF_AFR\t%INFO/AF_AMR\t%INFO/AF_EAS\t%INFO/AF_EUR\t%INFO/AF_SAS\n' /data/examples/variants/promoter_variants.vcf.gz > /home/user/promoter_variants.tsv
import pandas as pd
cols = ["chrom","pos","vid","ref","alt","gene","AF","AF_AFR","AF_AMR","AF_EAS","AF_EUR","AF_SAS"]
var = pd.read_csv("/home/user/promoter_variants.tsv", sep="\t", names=cols, na_values=".")
print(var.shape); var.head()
