Variants that hit an E-box

You now have two sets of positions on the genome: your E-boxes and the variants. The question is which variants fall inside an E-box, and that is a job for bedtools.

Finding overlaps with bedtools

bedtools intersect finds overlaps between two sets of genomic regions. Overlap your canonical E-boxes with the variants:

cd ~
bedtools intersect -a eboxes_canonical.bed -b /data/examples/variants/promoter_variants.vcf.gz -wa -wb \
  | head
# count the variants that fall in a canonical E-box:
bedtools intersect -a eboxes_canonical.bed -b /data/examples/variants/promoter_variants.vcf.gz -wb \
  | wc -l
Any change to CACGTG weakens it

CACGTG is the best possible sequence, so any single-base change inside it turns it into something weaker. Every SNV in a canonical E-box therefore weakens or destroys the site (you can check this with the PWM score in the next step). Occasionally a variant does the opposite and turns a near-match into a perfect CACGTG, creating a new site. Both kinds are interesting.

Build your catalogue in Python

Join the variants table (var, from the last step) to your E-boxes, so that each E-box variant comes with its gene and its population frequencies. A simple way to do this is to mark every genomic position inside a canonical E-box, then pick out the variants at those positions:

ebox_pos = set()
for chrom, s, e, gene in eboxes:          # from the find step (BED: 0-based s, end e)
    for p in range(s+1, e+1):             # 1-based genomic positions
        ebox_pos.add((chrom, p))

var["in_ebox"] = [ (c,p) in ebox_pos for c,p in zip(var.chrom, var.pos) ]
catalogue = var[var.in_ebox].copy()
print("variants inside a canonical E-box:", len(catalogue))
catalogue.sort_values("AF", ascending=False).head(10)

Look at the population columns

For each variant in the catalogue, compare AF_AFR … AF_SAS. Are any of them common in one population and rare in another? A variant that disrupts a clock E-box but is common in some population deserves a closer look.

cols = ["gene","pos","ref","alt","AF","AF_AFR","AF_AMR","AF_EAS","AF_EUR","AF_SAS"]
catalogue[cols].sort_values("AF", ascending=False).head(12)
Checkpoint

27 variants fall inside a canonical E-box. Most of them are rare, which is what you would expect if disrupting an E-box is usually mildly harmful, so that selection keeps such variants at low frequency. You will test this properly in Part 4.
