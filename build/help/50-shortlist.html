Your shortlist of candidates

You now have, for every promoter variant: whether it hits an E-box, how much it changes the motif score, how conserved the position is, and how common it is. Combine these into a ranked shortlist — the variants worth taking into the patient data.

What makes a strong candidate

  • It really disrupts the switch — in a canonical E-box, or a large negative PWM score change.
  • The position is conserved — high phyloP, so evolution cares about it.
  • It is in a relevant gene — a channelopathy or epilepsy gene, ideally your close-focus 21.
  • Its frequency is informative — rare variants fit a "damaging" story; a common one that still disrupts a conserved switch is a puzzle worth explaining.

Build the ranking

Join your motif-effect table (eff) to conservation and gene set, score, and sort. One simple, defensible score:

import pandas as pd
chan = set(pd.read_csv("/data/examples/genes/brain_channelopathy.tsv", sep="\t")["gene"])
ph = phylop.reset_index().rename(columns={"phyloP470":"phylop"})
cand = eff[eff["class"].isin(["break","weaken"])].merge(ph[["chrom","pos","phylop"]], on=["chrom","pos"], how="left")
cand["channelopathy"] = cand.gene.isin(chan)
cand["score"] = ( (-cand["delta"])              # bigger disruption = higher
                  + cand["phylop"].clip(lower=0)/3
                  + cand["channelopathy"]*1.0 )
shortlist = cand.sort_values("score", ascending=False).head(25)
shortlist[["gene","chrom","pos","ref","alt","AF","delta","phylop","channelopathy"]]
What a good shortlist looks like

Your top rows should be disrupting variants in conserved positions of channelopathy or epilepsy genes. In our run, candidates in genes such as CHD4, CUX2, ARFGEF2, TRPM7 rose to the top. There is no single right ranking — the point is that your rule is explicit and reproducible, so someone else can see why a variant is on the list.

Sanity-check your top hit

Take your number-one candidate and look at it three ways: its line in the VCF (frequencies), its position in the genome browser (is it really in a promoter E-box?), and the gene in Ensembl (what does the gene do?).

What happens next (beyond this page)

In the real project these candidates would be tested in the 100,000 Genomes Project epilepsy patients, inside Genomics England's secure environment: are disrupting E-box variants seen more often, or alongside more severe disease? That work needs information-governance training and is not done here — but your shortlist, and the reproducible method behind it, is exactly what makes it possible.
