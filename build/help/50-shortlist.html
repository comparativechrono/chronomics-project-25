Your shortlist of candidates

For every promoter variant you now know whether it is in an E-box, how much it changes the motif score, how conserved its position is and how common it is. Combine these into a ranked shortlist of the variants most worth following up in patient data.

What makes a strong candidate

  • It clearly disrupts the site: it is in a canonical E-box, or it causes a large drop in PWM score.
  • The position is conserved: a high phyloP score suggests evolution has kept it the same.
  • It is in a relevant gene: a channelopathy or epilepsy gene, ideally one of your 21 channelopathy genes.
  • Its frequency tells you something: rare variants fit the idea that disrupting the site is harmful, while a common variant that disrupts a conserved site is a puzzle worth explaining.

Build the ranking

Join your table of motif effects (eff) to the conservation scores and the gene list, give each variant a score, and sort. This is one simple scoring rule that is easy to justify:

import pandas as pd
chan = set(pd.read_csv("/data/examples/genes/brain_channelopathy.tsv", sep="\t")["gene"])
ph = phylop.reset_index().rename(columns={"phyloP470":"phylop"})
cand = eff[eff["class"].isin(["break","weaken"])].merge(ph[["chrom","pos","phylop"]], on=["chrom","pos"], how="left")
cand["channelopathy"] = cand.gene.isin(chan)
cand["score"] = ( (-cand["delta"])              # bigger disruption = higher
                  + cand["phylop"].clip(lower=0)/3
                  + cand["channelopathy"]*1.0 )
shortlist = cand.sort_values("score", ascending=False).head(25)
shortlist[["gene","chrom","pos","ref","alt","AF","delta","phylop","channelopathy"]]
What a good shortlist looks like

The top rows should be disrupting variants at conserved positions in channelopathy or epilepsy genes. When we ran it, candidates in genes such as CHD4, CUX2, ARFGEF2 and TRPM7 came near the top. There is no single right ranking. What matters is that your rule is explicit and reproducible, so that someone else can see why each variant is on the list.

Check your top candidate

Look at your top candidate in three ways: its line in the VCF (its frequencies), its position in the genome browser (is it really in a promoter E-box?), and the gene in Ensembl (what does the gene do?).

What happens next

In the full project, these candidates would be tested in the epilepsy patients of the 100,000 Genomes Project, inside Genomics England's secure environment. Are E-box-disrupting variants seen more often in patients, or in people with more severe disease? That work needs information governance training and isn't part of this guide, but it depends on a shortlist like yours, and on a reproducible method for making it.
