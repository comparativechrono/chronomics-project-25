Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms used in this project. You can search the guide (top left) to find where each one comes up.

Allele frequency (AF)
The fraction of chromosomes in a group that carry a particular variant. Low means rare and high means common.
BED file
A plain-text list of genomic regions (chromosome, start, end). Positions are counted from 0, and the end is one past the last base.
BMAL1 / CLOCK
The two proteins that pair up and switch on clock-controlled genes by binding E-boxes.
Channelopathy
A disease caused by a faulty ion channel. Many epilepsies are channelopathies.
Conservation
How little a DNA position has changed across species. Measured here with phyloP; a high score means the position is constrained.
CpG
A C followed by a G. CpGs mutate faster than other DNA and behave differently in other ways too, which is why comparisons need a CpG-matched control.
E-box
The DNA sequence that CLOCK:BMAL1 binds. The canonical E-box is the palindrome CACGTG.
Enrichment
Finding something more often than a fair estimate of chance would predict.
FASTA
A text format for DNA or protein sequences: a > header line, followed by the sequence.
GRCh38
The current human reference genome build. All the coordinates in this project are on GRCh38.
phyloP
A conservation score for each position, from comparing many species. Positive means conserved; negative means changing quickly.
Promoter
The stretch of DNA at the start of a gene that holds the sequences controlling it, including E-boxes.
Purifying selection
Evolution removing harmful changes, so that important positions stay conserved and carry little variation.
PWM (position weight matrix)
A flexible description of the sequences a protein binds, which allows near-matches as well as perfect ones. Here, sites are scored from 0 to 1.
SNV
A single-nucleotide variant: a change to one base of DNA.
TSS
Transcription start site: where a gene starts to be copied into RNA.
VCF
The standard file format for variants: position, reference base, alternative base and information fields.
